The mission

One flock. One drone. One bear.

The whole lab is one system: Pasture Sentinel, the drone that shepherds in the Pyrenees call for. It is small enough to hold in your head, and it has everything real systems have.

The goal of the lab

Four questions before anyone solders anything.

MBSE replaces the pile of documents with one model, built in layers. Each layer answers one question, and each is checked against the one before.

OperationalFunctionalLogicalPhysical

Operational

What is expected of the system?

Who is involved, and how does the mission unfold? You write the scenarios on a sunny day, then on a rainy day, when the flight plan is wrong.

Functional

How does it do it?

Functions, states, signals. The state machine is no drawing: it runs, and it moves the drone.

Logical

Which subsystem carries what?

You cut the drone into subsystems, and you say what they exchange. Three architectures, one choice, and you defend it. Still cheap to change.

Physical

What do we build, and what do we buy?

Each subsystem becomes real parts: a camera, a flight computer, a piece of software. For each one, one question: make it or buy it off the shelf? The requirements decide, and each part must prove it meets them.

Early validation

Find the mistake while it is still cheap.

A wrong assumption costs a click in a model, and a campaign once the drone is built. So the lab has you run your model at every step, long before there is anything to fly. Scroll, and watch the sheep climb.

Try it: you are the model.
