Model the intent, not the language.

The problem

The lock

The systems engineer wants to describe their system. MBSE asks them to master a language, a tool and a method, in exchange for a formalism you can automate, translate and analyse.

But modelling takes too much effort to build and to maintain, for too little benefit.

Step 1 · Operational layer

The need, written down by following a method

The engineer starts from what they have: requirements, needs, nominal and off-nominal scenarios, a context. They must transcribe it all into the model, step by step.

Step 1 · AI and human

The engineer describes the context, the AI generates it

The engineer explains the context elements and their connections in plain text. The AI writes, generates, completes the diagram, and checks its consistency.

The AI runs the method validators

Step 2 · Functional layer

The mission: a state machine, allocated functions

The black-box system has a mission state machine. Each state runs a function. Write it by hand, iterate it with the AI, or have it generated.

  • iterated with the AI

Step 2 · Activities

“When do we know this condition is true?”

The AI spots a transition with no condition and suggests a rule. The engineer agrees, the AI generates the activity diagram that computes it. The mission can now play by itself.

Step 2 · Simulation

The AI plays the scenarios in BEE and finds the gaps

Each scenario runs on the state machine, step by step. A summary report says what passes and which states were visited. A state never reached is a missing scenario, or a state too many.

AI

Step 2 · Full simulation

The black box, driven by its context

External systems have a behaviour too. The AI infers it for each actor, then every item goes through the system's ports. In BEE, the mission moves from phase to phase.

Step 3 · Logical layer · Gate 1

The AI generates, the gates judge

Inputs: the architecture drivers, the mission and the scenarios. The AI writes the architecture as a .sysml file and sends it through three gates in turn: SysML syntax, method, behaviour. Rejected? It reads why, corrects, and sends it again.

AI

Step 3 · Logical layer · Gate 2

The method finds what is not traced

The syntax is fine, now the method validator. A function was added but is not traced to the functional layer, an element takes part in no scenario. The AI regenerates the missing part on its own and sends it again.

Gate 2 · method validator
✗Function not traced: Estimate the flock size was added, but no function of the functional layer leads to it
✗Element in no scenario: Data logger takes part in none of the scenarios
AI

Step 3 · Logical layer · Gate 3

Every scenario, played at once

BEE plays all the scenarios in parallel. One fails: nobody receives the strong wind. A modelling slip or a design gap? That one is the engineer's call. The AI asks, takes the answer as a new requirement, updates the architecture and runs every gate again.

Result

A logical architecture you master

Let the AI run for 20 to 30 minutes. You get a logical architecture that meets the need, that you can review in SAM, and that you know holds up.

Engineering rigour, no longer modelling rigour.
